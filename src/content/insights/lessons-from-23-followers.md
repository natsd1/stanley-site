When I started on OdinBot, the X account had 23 followers. By the time the project was acquired by Cielo Finance, it had 4,600+, and the Discord had grown to 16,000 members with activity peaking at 10,000.

## Nobody is waiting for your announcement

With a tiny account, every post goes out into silence. That changes how you think. You stop writing "announcements" and start looking for conversations where you can be useful.

## Culture first, product close behind

Trading bots are a crowded, skeptical category. Feature lists don't cut through. What worked was showing up as part of the culture: memes, market chatter, quick takes. The product was always nearby, but rarely the opening line.

## Give the audience somewhere to go

Followers are fragile. Community members aren't. Moving people from X into Discord meant there was a place for questions, feedback and conversation. That's where the audience started to compound.

## Launches are sequences

Every launch was a story with beats: tease, reveal, demo, follow-up. One post is an announcement. A sequence is a moment.

## What I'd tell anyone starting from zero

- Spend more time in replies than on your own posts
- Sound like the people you want to reach
- Build the community early, before you think you need it
- Track who is following, not just how many
