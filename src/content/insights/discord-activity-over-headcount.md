A Discord with 50,000 members and three messages a day is not a community. It's a mailing list with worse open rates.

The number that matters is how many people show up and talk.

## Design the first five minutes

Most people decide whether to stay within minutes of joining. In that window they need to know:

- What this place is for
- Where to start
- That someone is actually here

Keep the onboarding short. A welcome message, one channel to introduce yourself, and a clear path to the main conversation. Twenty locked channels and a ten-step verification flow will lose people before they ever say hello.

## Give regulars a rhythm

Communities run on habits. Weekly events, recurring threads and predictable moments give people a reason to come back:

- A weekly AMA or Spaces with the team
- A recurring meme or alpha-sharing thread
- A "what are you building / trading" check-in

The format matters less than the consistency.

## Keep the team visible

Nothing kills a community faster than the feeling that the team has left. Founders and team members don't need to be online all day, but they need to show up regularly and answer real questions.

## Moderate early, moderate well

Scammers and spam move into quiet servers fast. A clear set of rules, a reliable mod team and fast responses keep the space safe enough for normal people to stick around.

## Listen and close the loop

Your community will tell you what's broken, what's confusing and what they want next. When you act on that feedback, tell them. "You asked, we shipped" is one of the strongest messages a project can send.
