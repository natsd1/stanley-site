Every Web3 project eventually hits the same wall. The follower count is climbing, the impressions look healthy, and the product dashboard hasn't moved.

That gap is where most social strategies quietly fail. Not because the content is bad, but because nobody designed the path from "I saw this post" to "I used this thing."

## Attention is step one, not the finish line

Attention is cheap to measure and easy to celebrate. But a follow is a very low-commitment action. It costs the user nothing. If your strategy ends at the follow, you're building an audience for someone else to monetise.

The better question is: **what's the next step after the follow, and is it obvious?**

## Map the path out loud

Before you plan another month of content, write the journey down:

1. Someone sees a post (where? from whom?)
2. They visit the profile (does the bio explain the product in one line?)
3. They check the pinned post (does it show the product working?)
4. They join the community or try the product (is the link there? is it one click?)
5. They come back (why would they?)

Most projects have strong content at step one and nothing at steps two through five.

## Give every content pillar a job

A simple way to fix this is to make sure your content mix covers the whole path:

- **Culture posts** earn attention. Memes, market takes, timely jokes.
- **Education posts** build understanding. What the product does, in plain language.
- **Proof posts** build trust. Real usage, real users, real numbers.
- **Product posts** convert. A clear reason to try it today.

If 90% of your feed is culture, you'll grow followers and stay invisible in your product metrics.

## Community is the bridge

The most reliable bridge between social and product is a community space. Discord, Telegram, whatever your users already use. It's where curious followers become people who ask questions, get answers and bring their friends.

A follower can scroll past you forever. A community member has a reason to come back.

## Measure the right thing

Track follower growth, sure. But put these next to it:

- Profile visits → link clicks
- Link clicks → community joins
- Community joins → active members
- Active members → product users

The moment you track the funnel instead of the headline number, your content strategy starts to look very different.
