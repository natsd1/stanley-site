Planning content in crypto feels pointless at first. You schedule a week of posts on Monday, and by Tuesday the whole market is talking about something else.

The fix isn't to stop planning. It's to plan differently.

## Split the calendar in two

Think of your content in two layers:

- **The backbone:** evergreen posts that explain the product, reinforce positioning and show proof. These can be planned weeks ahead.
- **The pulse:** reactive posts that respond to the market, the timeline and the culture. These can only be planned as slots, not content.

A healthy split for most projects is around 60/40. Enough structure to stay consistent, enough room to stay relevant.

## Plan slots, not just posts

Instead of filling every day with fixed content, reserve windows:

- One or two "react" slots per day for whatever the timeline is talking about
- A weekly slot for a longer thread or explainer
- A recurring community moment, like a Spaces or AMA

When the news cycle hits, you're not tearing up the plan. You're filling the slots you already left open.

## Keep a ready bank

Build a small library of posts that work any week: product explainers, FAQs, user wins, memes that fit your brand. When a quiet day comes, you're never staring at a blank draft.

## Review weekly, not monthly

Monthly reviews are too slow for this market. Every week, look at:

- Which posts drove the most profile visits
- Which formats got real replies, not just likes
- Which topics the community kept bringing up

Then adjust next week's backbone.

## The rule that matters most

Never let the calendar stop you from saying something timely. A plan is there to keep you consistent, not to keep you quiet.
